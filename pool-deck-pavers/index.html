Why homeowners redo pool decks

  • Cracked or heaving concrete, often from settling or tree roots.
  • A painted or textured coating that is peeling and needs redoing every few years.
  • A deck that gets too hot to walk on barefoot.
  • Slippery spots, rust stains or an outdated look before selling the house.

Travertine or concrete pavers

Travertine is the most popular choice around Florida pools. It stays noticeably cooler underfoot than most concrete, its natural texture helps with grip when wet, and it suits a coastal look. Concrete pavers cost less and come in more colors. Lighter colors stay cooler in either material.

Overlay or tear-out

If the existing concrete deck is solid and mostly level, many companies can lay thinner pavers or travertine directly over it with a bonded mortar or sand bed. That saves tear-out work. If the deck is cracked, sinking or lifted by roots, it usually has to come out first. Ask each company to explain which approach they recommend for your deck and why.

Coping and the pool edge

Coping is the cap around the pool edge. It is often replaced at the same time as the deck so the colors and heights match. Ask how the company will protect the pool finish and tile during the work, and whether the coping is set on mortar.

What affects the price

  • Deck size in square feet.
  • Material. Travertine usually costs more than concrete pavers.
  • Overlay or tear-out of the old concrete.
  • Coping replacement and any step or bench edges.
  • Screen enclosure. Working inside a cage takes more care, and footers may need adjusting.

Before you hire anyone

  • Ask for proof of liability insurance and workers' compensation.
  • Get the quote in writing, including coping, drainage, sealing and cleanup.
  • Ask how they handle water draining off the deck and away from the pool.
  • Ask about the workmanship warranty.

Pool Deck Pavers questions

Do pavers get hot around a pool?

Every surface warms up in the Florida sun, but light-colored travertine stays noticeably cooler than dark pavers or plain concrete. Ask to stand on a sample in direct sun before you choose.

Are pavers slippery when wet?

Tumbled travertine and textured pavers give good grip. Some sealers make the surface slicker, so ask about a non-slip additive if the deck will be sealed.

Do I have to drain the pool?

Usually not. The company works around the edge and protects the water and finish. If coping is being replaced, they may lower the water level a little.

How long does a pool deck take?

Many pool decks are done in under a week once work starts. Tear-out of an old slab and coping replacement add time.
